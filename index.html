Abstract

Access to safe drinking water is essential for human health and sustainable development. However, water contamination from microorganisms, industrial waste and agricultural chemicals can create serious public health risks. This paper examines common sources of drinking water contamination, considers methods used to improve water quality, and discusses the importance of effective water treatment and monitoring. It also highlights the role of water-quality standards in protecting communities.

1. Introduction

Drinking water is necessary for human survival, sanitation and public health. Although water may appear clean, it can contain contaminants that are not visible to the naked eye. These contaminants can enter water supplies through agricultural runoff, industrial discharge, sewage and deteriorating infrastructure.

Water contamination is an important environmental engineering issue because the quality of drinking water depends on the effectiveness of treatment processes, distribution systems and routine monitoring.

2. Analysis and Discussion

2.1 Sources of Contamination

Drinking water can be affected by biological, chemical and physical contaminants. Biological contaminants include bacteria, viruses and parasites. Chemical contaminants may include pesticides, heavy metals and industrial chemicals. Sediment and suspended particles can also affect water quality.

2.2 Water Treatment Methods

Water treatment facilities use different processes to remove contaminants. Coagulation and filtration help remove suspended particles, while disinfection can reduce the risk posed by harmful microorganisms. The choice of treatment method depends on the source water and the contaminants that need to be removed.

2.3 Public Health Implications

Unsafe drinking water can contribute to waterborne illnesses and other health problems. Regular monitoring, suitable treatment systems and the protection of water sources are important measures for reducing these risks.

4. Conclusion

Drinking water contamination remains an important environmental and public health concern. Effective water treatment, protection of water sources and routine monitoring can help reduce the risks associated with contaminated supplies. Understanding the strengths and limitations of different treatment processes is essential when developing reliable approaches to water safety.
